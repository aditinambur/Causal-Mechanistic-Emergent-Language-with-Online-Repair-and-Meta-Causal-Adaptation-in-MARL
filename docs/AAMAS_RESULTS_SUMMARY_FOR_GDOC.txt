--- 1. EXPERIMENTAL SUMMARY PARAGRAPH ---

To evaluate whether decentralized multi-agent teams can diagnose and repair broken communication protocols online under distribution shift without full team retraining, we evaluated MAPPO agents learning emergent communication (V=5, d=64) in the MPE simple_spread cooperative navigation environment across two contention scales (2 agents/3 landmarks and 3 agents/4 landmarks). Under Common Random Numbers (CRN) counterfactual message ablation (do(m=0)), mean communication utility (Delta R_comm) increased from +488.4 (2 agents) to +925.4 (3 agents; +1146.6 on Seed 1 N=20 eval), and receiver behavioral steering (CIC KL divergence) increased from 0.05 to 0.10 (up to 0.87), confirming higher communication reliance under coordination bottlenecks. When controlled observation coordinate shifts (partner_full mirror inversion) corrupted communication semantics, our dual-gated meta-causal detector correctly triggered repair on genuine protocol collapses (e.g., rho_causal = 1.91) while frugally abstaining on non-communicative and noise-induced dips (<10% drop), whereas naive reward-only triggers missed severe breakdowns. Surgical Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) tuning just 1,293 weights (<1.5% of policy capacity) for 15 update iterations (<1% of retraining time) recovered 78.5% of lost reward (up to 105.5%) in-sample and generalized to fresh, unseen layouts (66.2% to 102.8% recovery across 20 held-out episodes) while preserving >97.3% retention on the clean task. Crucially, a size-matched non-communicative motor ablation (noncomm_repair) failed held-out validation in 100% of cases (-2.7% and -50.9% recovery), and forced fine-tuning revealed agents can cheat task reward without fixing communication (18.2% comm recovery), proving that causal communication gating and dual acceptance are strictly necessary to salvage multi-agent coordination.
